Chapter six

Find out what people actually want.

Clients rarely know what they want, and users rarely say what they need. Research is how designers stop guessing. Here's how professionals uncover the colours, styles and feelings a project needs before drawing anything.

6.1 · The big picture

Design research has three targets

The business

What does the client need to achieve? More sales, a new audience, looking more premium?

The people

Who is it for? What do they care about, fear and already love? Where will they see it?

The market

What are competitors doing? What's overused? Where's the empty space to stand in?

Good design sits where all three overlap. A gorgeous logo that ignores the audience fails. A safe logo that looks like every competitor fails too.

Explain like I'm 10

Before you buy a birthday present, you find out what your friend likes, what you can afford, and what everyone else is already giving them. That's market research!

6.2 · How people decide

People are bad at telling you what they want. Watch instead.

Say ≠ do

Economists call it stated vs revealed preference. People say they want "clean and minimal" and then click the loud, colourful one. Trust behaviour over opinions.

Jobs to be done

Clayton Christensen's idea: people "hire" products to do a job. His team found many morning milkshakes were bought to make a boring commute bearable. Ask what job your design is hired for.

Faster horses

"If I'd asked people what they wanted, they'd have said faster horses." It's usually credited to Henry Ford, though there's no proof he said it. The point stands: ask about problems, not solutions.

The Kano model: three kinds of wants

Professor Noriaki Kano (1984) showed that features affect happiness in different ways:

Must-have

Basic needs

Nobody thanks you for them, but everyone's angry if they're missing. Readable text, working buttons, correct logo.

More = better

Performance

The better you do, the happier people get. Faster, clearer, easier to find.

Wow

Delighters

Unexpected extras: a lovely animation, a witty line, a hidden detail. These make people talk about you. Today's delighter is tomorrow's basic.

Research · You only need ~5 users

Nielsen & Landauer's model suggests testing with about 5 users uncovers most usability problems (roughly 85% in their model). Several small rounds beat one big test.

Nielsen, J. & Landauer, T. K. (1993). A mathematical model of the finding of usability problems. Proceedings of ACM INTERCHI'93.

6.5 · The brief

Every project starts with a brief

A brief is a one-page agreement on what you're making and why. No brief means endless revisions. Copy this template and use it for every project, including personal ones.

PROJECT BRIEF ————————————————————— 1. The client / brand Who are they? One sentence. 2. The problem What isn't working right now? Why now? 3. The goal What does success look like? (more sign-ups, look premium, reach Gen Z...) 4. The audience Who exactly? Age, place, habits, what they already love. 5. Personality — 3 to 5 words e.g. warm · honest · quick-witted NOT: corporate · cold · cheap 6. Competitors & references 3 competitors + 3 brands (any industry) they admire. 7. Deliverables Logo, colour palette, type, social templates, packaging... 8. Must-haves / must-avoids Mandatory elements, colours to avoid, cultural notes. 9. Timeline & budget Key dates, number of revision rounds. 10. How we'll decide Who approves? What are we measuring against? (→ the goal + the personality words, not "I like it")
6.6 · The design job market

Where the work is heading

$61.3kUS median pay, graphic designers (May 2024, BLS)
$98.1kUS median pay, web & digital interface designers (May 2024, BLS)
+2%Projected growth for graphic design jobs, 2024–34. Slower than average.
+7%Projected growth for web & digital interface design, 2024–34. Faster than average.

What this means for you: pure print-only graphic design is shrinking. Design that lives on screens, in systems and in motion is growing. Learn the fundamentals in this guide (they never expire), then aim them at digital work.

Brand identity

Logos, colour and type systems, guidelines. Highest creative status, very competitive.

UI / product design

Apps and websites. Best pay and growth. Needs graphic skills plus UX thinking.

Marketing & social

Ads, social posts, campaigns. Lots of jobs and fast-paced. A great first job.

Motion design

Animated logos, explainer videos, UI animation. Rising demand, fewer people do it well.

Packaging

Physical products on shelves. Combines branding, print and 3D thinking.

Illustration

Custom drawings for brands, editorial and products. The antidote to stock-photo sameness.

Reality check · AI and design jobs

Adobe's analysis of BLS wage-survey data shows counted graphic-design employment fell from about 214k (May 2024) to about 198k (May 2025), while the median wage rose. Routine production work (resizing, simple social posts) is being automated. Taste, strategy, research and systems thinking are what clients still pay humans for. That is exactly what this guide trains.

Sources: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook & OEWS (May 2024); Adobe, "The Economic State of Creative Professions" (2026).
