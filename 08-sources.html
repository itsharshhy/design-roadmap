How to read the ratings

Not all "research" is equal

Strong

Replicated many times or official data. Build on it.

Moderate

Solid peer-reviewed studies, but limited samples or contexts. Use with judgement.

Guideline / theory

Expert frameworks and standards. Useful rules of thumb, not lab proof.

Use with care

Popular but over-stated, anecdotal or untraceable. Quote carefully.

Ch 02 · PsychologyStrong

You have 50 milliseconds

Ratings of a web page's visual appeal after a 50 ms flash closely matched ratings after 500 ms. The gut verdict on looks is near-instant. It measured appeal only, not trust or buying.

Lindgaard, Fernandes, Dudek & Brown (2006) · Behaviour & Information Technology 25(2) · 3 studies

Simple + familiar looks beautiful

Sites with low visual complexity and high "prototypicality" (looking like what people expect) were rated most appealing, even at 17–50 ms exposures.

Tuch, Presslaber, Stöcklin, Opwis & Bargas-Avila (2012) · Int. J. Human-Computer Studies 70(11)

Looks drive credibility

In comments from 2,684 people comparing live sites, 46.1% of comments mentioned "design look" (layout, type, colour), more than any other factor. Comment analysis, not a controlled experiment.

Fogg et al. (2002) · Stanford Web Credibility Project / Consumer WebWatch

Mere-exposure effect

We like things more the more we see them. It's one of psychology's most replicated effects, and it's why your diet of images shapes your taste.

Zajonc (1968) · J. Personality & Social Psychology 9(2) · later meta-analysis: Bornstein (1989)

Pre-attentive pop-out

Single features like colour or orientation "pop out" in parallel; combinations need slow serial search. Make the key thing different in one obvious way.

Treisman & Gelade (1980) · Cognitive Psychology 12(1)

Gestalt grouping

Proximity, similarity, closure, continuity and figure/ground describe how we perceive wholes. Over a century of perception research builds on them.

Ch 02 · PsychologyStrong

Hick's Law

Decision time rises with the number of choices (logarithmically). Strong in the lab; in real interfaces, familiarity and grouping matter too.

Hick (1952) · Quarterly J. Experimental Psychology 4(1)

Ch 02 · PsychologyStrong

Fitts's Law

Time to hit a target depends on its distance and size. Big, near buttons are faster. Among the most robust laws in HCI.

Fitts (1954) · J. Experimental Psychology 47(6)

Ch 02 · PsychologyStrong

Working memory is small

Miller suggested 7±2 items; later work by Cowan points to about 4 chunks. Chunk information and don't make people remember things between screens.

Miller (1956) · Psychological Review 63(2); Cowan (2001) · Behavioral & Brain Sciences 24(1)

Isolation (von Restorff) effect

An item that differs from its neighbours is remembered better. That's the logic behind one highlighted plan or one red button.

von Restorff (1933) · Psychologische Forschung 18

Before you quote a stat

Five questions that make you un-fooled-able

  1. Who said it, and where?

    A journal, a book or a blog? Can you find the original, not just a blog quoting a blog?

  2. What kind of evidence?

    Experiment, survey, review, case study or opinion? Each answers a different question.

  3. How many people?

    20 students in one university is a hint. 2,000 people across countries is a pattern.

  4. Has anyone repeated it?

    Findings that replicate (like mere exposure or Fitts's Law) are worth building on.

  5. Does it fit my context?

    A colour result from US shoppers may not hold in Mumbai. Context changes meaning.
